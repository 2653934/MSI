\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:data}
Three public MSI collections were used (\cref{tab:data,fig:data}). They are the
collections on which S\textsuperscript{3}PL was
published~\citep{weigand2026spatial}, so both baselines could be reproduced
under their own protocols and then compared with our methods under a common
one.

\textbf{GBM} is the massNet patient-derived xenograft (PDX) glioblastoma
collection, acquired by MALDI on a 9.4\,T FT-ICR
instrument~\citep{abdelmoula2022massnet}. It is available from the
Metabolomics Workbench (project PR001292, study ST002045,
doi:10.21228/M8Q70T). It contains eight sections from four PDX lines, two per
line, with pathologist annotations of tumour and normal tissue. Sections from
the same line are not independent, so GBM results are also summarised per
line. \textbf{CAC} is a DESI collection of 52 serial sections from one human
colorectal adenocarcinoma~\citep{inglese2017deep}, available from MetaboLights
(MTBLS415). We used the eight sections and three-class masks released with
S\textsuperscript{3}PL~\citep{weigand2026spatial}, so CAC comparisons are
within one specimen. \textbf{RCC} is the DESI renal cell carcinoma collection
of \citet{dill2010rcc}, distributed with the Bioconductor package
CardinalWorkflows~\citep{bemis2015cardinal}. It contains eight runs, each a
matched pair of cancer and normal tissue from one subject. Pixels outside the
annotated tissue have no label; this unannotated region covers 45--78\% of
each run. The dataset's own workflow treats it as background, but this
interpretation was not independently verified. We kept it as a
third class in the primary analysis and report a cancer/normal-only
sensitivity analysis (\cref{sec:res-rcc}). Measured coordinates and masks were
audited for alignment in every section.

serves as a held-out confirmation collection.

\subsection{Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
\Cref{fig:pipeline} summarises the pipeline. For each section, a model is
fitted to that section's spectra without labels, peaks are ranked from the
fitted model, and the selected peaks are then scored against that section's
expert masks. The design is therefore unsupervised and transductive. The masks
never enter model fitting or ranking. They do, however, set two configuration
values, the number of segments and the peak budget, both disclosed below.

\centering

  figure}}}}
\caption{Pipeline. A VAE is fitted to one section's spectra, with or without
neighbourhood context; its latent means are segmented by a GMM; and $m/z$ bins
are ranked either by Integrated Gradients through the encoder or by
correlation of each ion image with the GMM posterior maps. Model-free rankings
use the spectra directly. Expert masks are used only for scoring.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure*}

\textbf{Input.} Let $\mathbf{s}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{D}_{\ge0}$ be the spectrum at
pixel $i$ over $D$ bins. Spectra are normalised by total ion current (TIC),
$\mathbf{x}_i=\mathbf{s}_i/\sum_{d}s_{id}$. For the context arms, let
$\mathcal{N}(i)$ be the measured pixels among the eight neighbours of $i$ in a
$3\times3$ window. The uniform context is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{c}_i=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}(i)|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\mathbf{x}_j ,
\end{equation}
and the encoder input is $\mathbf{u}_i=\mathbf{x}_i$ (centre-only) or
$\mathbf{u}_i=[\mathbf{x}_i;\mathbf{c}_i]$ (uniform). Pixels at tissue edges
use only their measured neighbours, so no unmeasured location is padded.

\textbf{Model.} Following msiPL~\citep{abdelmoula2021peak}, the VAE has one
hidden layer of 512 units with batch normalisation and a 5-dimensional latent
space~\citep{kingma2014autoencoding}:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{h}_i &= \mathrm{ReLU}\big(\mathrm{BN}(W_e\mathbf{u}_i+\mathbf{b}_e)\big),\\
\boldsymbol{\mu}_i &= W_\mu\mathbf{h}_i,\quad
\log\boldsymbol{\sigma}^2_i = W_\sigma\mathbf{h}_i,\quad
\mathbf{z}_i=\boldsymbol{\mu}_i+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_i\odot\boldsymbol{\epsilon},
\end{align}
with $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},I)$. The decoder mirrors
the encoder and outputs $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_i=\mathrm{sigmoid}(W_o\,
\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{BN}(W_d\mathbf{z}_i)))$, a reconstruction of the centre
spectrum only. With $p_{id}=\hat{x}_{id}/\sum_{d'}\hat{x}_{id'}$, the loss is
msiPL's categorical cross-entropy plus the Kullback--Leibler divergence to a
standard normal prior:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_i=-D\sum_{d}x_{id}\log p_{id}
-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{5}\big(1+\log\sigma^2_{il}-\mu_{il}^2-\sigma^2_{il}\big).
\end{equation}
Training uses Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 128) for 100 epochs.
For GBM the centre-only and uniform models have 87.2M and 130.8M parameters,
almost all in the first layer. Further variants replace the uniform mean with
learned single-head attention over the neighbours~\citep{vaswani2017attention}
(about 0.5\% more parameters), use $5\times5$, $7\times7$ or $9\times9$
windows, replace the neighbours with zeros or with spectra shuffled from distant
pixels, or hide the centre spectrum in half of the training samples so that the
neighbours become necessary. Each variant was compared with the uniform arm
trained under the same seed.

\textbf{Segmentation.} The latent means $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i$ are standardised
and a Gaussian mixture model with full covariance is fitted to them. Its
posterior
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{ik}=\frac{\pi_k\,\mathcal{N}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i\mid\mathbf{m}_k,\Sigma_k)}
{\sum_{l=1}^{K}\pi_l\,\mathcal{N}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i\mid\mathbf{m}_l,\Sigma_l)}
\end{equation}
gives, for each component $k$, a posterior map over the section. The number of
components $K$ equals the number of annotated classes (\cref{tab:data}). This
is a class-informed choice; the Bayesian information criterion
(BIC)~\citep{schwarz1978estimating} did not support it
(\cref{sec:res-encoder}). The pipeline is therefore label-free in fitting and
ranking but not fully autonomous.

\textbf{Rankings.} Every ranking orders all $D$ bins, and the top
$K_{\text{bin}}$ bins are scored. The two model-based rankings use the same
fitted GMM. For both, a score is computed per component and the per-component
orderings are merged round-robin, skipping repeated bins, so that every
component contributes equally (``balanced'').
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Integrated Gradients} attributes component $k$'s posterior at
        pixel $i$ to each input bin along a straight path from a baseline
        $\mathbf{u}'$, the section's mean input~\citep{sundararajan2017axiomatic}:
        \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{IG}_{d}(\mathbf{u}_i)=(u_{id}-u'_d)\int_0^1
        \frac{\partial\gamma_k\big(\mathbf{u}'+\alpha(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}')\big)}
        {\partial u_{d}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha ,
        \end{equation}
        approximated with 64 trapezoidal steps for 12 sampled pixels assigned
        to each component. Attributions to a bin's centre and context inputs
        are combined, and bins are scored by mean absolute attribution.
        Attributions must satisfy completeness,
        $\sum_d\mathrm{IG}_d\approx\gamma_k(\mathbf{u}_i)-\gamma_k(\mathbf{u}')$.
        Where this check failed (4 of 144 runs), the run was recomputed from
        its saved model with 128 or 256 steps; in four control runs, doubling
        the steps changed mSCF1 by at most 0.0003.
  \item \emph{Posterior correlation} scores bin $d$ for component $k$ by
        $|\rho(I_d,\gamma_{\cdot k})|$, the absolute Pearson correlation over
        pixels between the bin's ion image $I_d$ and the component's posterior
        map. It needs no gradients; this is the segment-then-correlate
        approach~\citep{alexandrov2011efficient}.
  \item \emph{Model-free rankings} use the spectra directly: mean intensity,
        variance and global Moran's~$I$~\citep{moran1950notes},
        \begin{equation}
        I_d=\frac{N}{\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}}
        \frac{\sum_{i,j}w_{ij}(x_{id}-\bar{x}_d)(x_{jd}-\bar{x}_d)}
        {\sum_i(x_{id}-\bar{x}_d)^2},
        \end{equation}
        with $w_{ij}=1$ for measured Moore neighbours and 0 otherwise.
  \item \emph{References:} a random ranking, and a supervised oracle that ranks
        bins by correlation with the expert masks. The oracle is a ceiling only.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Controls on the representation and segmentation.} A \emph{PCA control}
replaces the VAE latent space with a 5-component PCA while keeping the GMM and
posterior correlation, which isolates the nonlinear encoder. A \emph{smoothed
segmentation} smooths the latent means with structure-adaptive neighbourhood
weights before the GMM~\citep{alexandrov2011efficient}, which places spatial
information at the segmentation step instead of the encoder input.

\textbf{Baselines.} Legacy msiPL was reproduced from its released code, with
its peak-selection threshold tuned towards the published peak counts as in the
original procedure~\citep{abdelmoula2021peak}. S\textsuperscript{3}PL was run
from the public repository (v1.0.0) with the released settings: 10 epochs,
Adam (learning rate $10^{-2}$), batch size 16, and patch size and peaks per
patch of $p=3$, $z=256$ (GBM), $p=9$, $z=256$ (CAC) and $p=5$, $z=1024$
(RCC)~\citep{weigand2026spatial}. The released code normalises each patch by
its spatial maximum, whereas the paper describes TIC normalisation, so both
were tested (\cref{sec:res-s3pl}).

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
\textbf{mSCF1.} For each class mask and threshold $t\in\{0.3,0.4,0.5,0.6\}$, a
reference set $R_t$ of bins is formed from the bins whose ion images correlate
with the mask, using the threshold-derived rank cutoff of the released
S\textsuperscript{3}PL evaluator~\citep{weigand2026spatial}. For a selected
set $S$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{mSCF1}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{t}\frac{2\,|S\cap R_t|}{|S|+|R_t|},
\end{equation}
with $R_t$ the union of the class reference sets. mSCF1 measures spatial
agreement with expert annotation, not chemical identity.

\textbf{Matched budgets.} All methods select the same number of bins,
$K_{\text{bin}}$, per section, so that differences reflect which bins are
chosen and not how many. For GBM and CAC, $K_{\text{bin}}$ is the tuned legacy
msiPL peak count; for RCC it is the released S\textsuperscript{3}PL target,
which approximately equals the number of bins correlating with the masks at
$t=0.5$. Budgets of $0.5\times$ to $2\times K_{\text{bin}}$ were checked as a
sensitivity analysis. For msiPL, its saved weights were re-thresholded over
$\beta=-2.0$ to $2.5$ (step 0.01) and, at each budget, the list whose length was
closest to the budget was taken; every ranking was then scored at that list's
own length. On RCC the longest msiPL list (1{,}054--1{,}289 bins in six of the
eight runs) was shorter than $K_{\text{bin}}$, so those points use the longest
list and are flagged; on GBM, seven of the smallest budgets were below the
shortest msiPL list and are flagged likewise.

\textbf{Peak-level scoring (P1).} Neighbouring bins of one molecular peak would
otherwise each count as a true positive. P1 therefore collapses bins within
one basin of the section's mean spectrum into a single peak group, and
partitions were audited and approved before any score was computed. On CAC and
RCC, P1 groups are singletons or near-singletons, so P1 acts there as a second
budget check rather than independent evidence.

\subsection{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}
Each comparison in \cref{sec:results} follows a rule written down before its
outputs existed, in an append-only protocol that also records deviations and
execution logs. The rules differ by comparison. In the context comparisons,
a method \emph{helps} if its mean gain is at least $+0.02$ mSCF1 and it gains
in at least 6 of 8 sections; methods are \emph{equivalent} if their mean
difference lies within $\pm0.02$. Other complete outcomes are \emph{mixed}.
The primary IG-versus-correlation rule instead asks whether IG is lower in at
least 7 of 8 sections in every run; its predeclared labels and fall-through
cases are reported as written. Missing arms remain incomplete, not mixed.
The threshold of 0.02 was chosen as the smallest difference we would act on.
The two core arms were trained with
three seeds on all 24 sections; the variants were screened against the uniform
arm with one seed each. Seed results are reported as the mean $\pm$ SD across
seeds of the collection means.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
\label{sec:stats}
The outcome labels are accompanied by paired non-parametric statistics over
sections: the exact two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank
test~\citep{wilcoxon1945individual}, the Hodges--Lehmann (HL) median paired
difference with its exact 95\% confidence interval~\citep{hodges1963estimates},
the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation $r$ and the sign count. Every pair
of methods is scored on the same sections, so the data are paired and the
signed-rank test is used rather than the unpaired Mann--Whitney test, which
would ignore that pairing. Where three seeds exist, seeds are averaged within
each section first, so the unit is the section rather than the run.

The 38 rule-labelled comparisons were grouped into five families by question:
ranking (6 tests), versus legacy msiPL (4), encoder context and variants (17),
segmentation (9) and S\textsuperscript{3}PL (2). Holm
correction~\citep{holm1979simple} was applied within each family. With $n=8$
the smallest attainable exact p-value is $2/2^8=0.0078$, so Holm significance
is attainable only in families of at most six tests, and effect sizes and
intervals carry the main weight. A comparison is called \emph{statistically
supported} only if its Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ \emph{and} its outcome label
points the same way. GBM is also summarised per PDX line ($n=4$), outside the
Holm families. Mixed-effects models with random intercepts for section and
seed are reported descriptively.

\subsection{Implementation and computation}
\label{sec:compute}
The pipeline was implemented in Python with PyTorch and
scikit-learn~\citep{pedregosa2011scikit}. Training ran on the University's
MSCluster under Slurm, on RTX 3060 and RTX 3090 GPUs (all timed training on RTX
3090), and ranking and scoring
ran on CPU nodes. The code, protocol, result summaries and figure scripts are
available at \url{https://github.com/2653934/Spatial-msiPL}\checkme{release tag
and commit}.

\textbf{Caching.} The original data loader re-read each batch from HDF5 on
shared storage, and a 100-epoch GBM run took 11.3\,h. Profiling showed that
preparing a batch took 24.6\,s, while the forward and backward passes took
about 0.02\,s. Reading each section into host memory once (about 13\,s)
reduced the run to 23\,min, a $30\times$ speed-up, with identical loss
histories and final weights. The cache costs one float32 copy of the section,
$N\times D\times4$ bytes: 0.70\,GB for GBM108\_positive and at most 1.54\,GB for
the largest GBM section, with GPU memory unchanged. The likely cause of the
slowdown is many small reads over network storage, which was not measured
separately.

\textbf{Reproducibility checks.} Before saved runs were reused, same-seed reruns
were checked for exact equality of losses, scores and selected bins. Exact
equality of rankings depends on the CPU model, because floating-point
summation order differs between processors, so each ranking was timed on the
same processor type that produced it. Saved result summaries record evaluator
and protocol versions, parameters, input hashes and code hashes, while scripts
refuse stale or existing outputs; exact reproduction remains hardware- and
path-sensitive.
