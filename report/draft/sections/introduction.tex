\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Mass spectrometry imaging (MSI) records a full mass spectrum at every pixel of
a tissue section, so the spatial distribution of many molecules can be studied
at once without choosing targets in
advance~\citep{caprioli1997maldi,schwamborn2010molecular,buchberger2018mass}.
The resulting data are large and high-dimensional. The three collections used
here, glioblastoma (GBM), colorectal adenocarcinoma (CAC) and renal cell
carcinoma (RCC), contain 972 to 4{,}761 pixels per section and between 1{,}481
and 85{,}062 mass-to-charge ($m/z$) bins per spectrum. Most
analyses therefore begin by selecting a small set of informative peaks.

Classical \emph{peak picking} applies smoothing, normalisation and
signal-to-noise thresholds to each spectrum on its
own~\citep{gibb2012maldiquant,deininger2011normalization}. It depends on
manually set thresholds, can discard weak but informative
ions~\citep{lieb2020sparse} and ignores \emph{where} in the tissue a signal
occurs. \emph{Peak learning} instead treats selection as an unsupervised
representation-learning problem: a model is fitted to the spectra of a section
and $m/z$ bins are ranked by how the model uses
them~\citep{li2022machinelearningpeakpicking,abdelmoula2021peak}. msiPL trains a
variational autoencoder (VAE) on individual spectra and reads peaks from its
weights~\citep{abdelmoula2021peak}. S\textsuperscript{3}PL adds spatial information by convolving
over small tissue patches and reconstructing the whole
neighbourhood, and reports large gains over msiPL~\citep{weigand2026spatial}.

S\textsuperscript{3}PL changes the architecture, the objective, the normalisation and the
selection rule at the same time, so it is not clear which ingredient produces
its gain. It also leaves open whether cheaper local context, such as the
mean spectrum of a pixel's neighbours, can give a lightweight VAE a similar
benefit, and, once a model has been fitted, which part of the pipeline actually
determines peak quality. We study this with
\emph{Spatial-msiPL}, an msiPL-style VAE that can encode a spectrum together
with its neighbourhood. Its latent space is segmented with a Gaussian mixture
model (GMM), and peaks are ranked either by Integrated Gradients
(IG)~\citep{sundararajan2017axiomatic} through the model or by correlating each
ion image with the GMM posterior maps. Specifically, we ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*}]
  \item Does ranking peaks from a segmentation of the VAE latent space select
        better peaks than legacy msiPL's weight-based selection when both select
        the same number of bins?
  \item Which ranking mechanism on the segmentation determines peak quality,
        gradient attribution or direct correlation, and does the segmentation
        need a nonlinear encoder?
  \item Where does spatial information help: as input to the encoder, in the
        segmentation or in the ranking statistic?
\end{enumerate}
RQ1 checks that the pipeline is worth studying; RQ2 and RQ3 locate the source
of any gain, which a single headline score against a baseline cannot do. To
make these comparisons trustworthy, we also reproduce both baselines and
analyse how sensitive S\textsuperscript{3}PL is to the training seed.

The main ranking comparisons used matched bin counts per section and the same
fair-scoring evaluator. Their decision rules were written down before the
corresponding results existed, and the core model comparisons were repeated
over three training seeds. The msiPL and S\textsuperscript{3}PL reproductions were
first assessed under their own protocols and evaluators, separately from the
common scoring used for all other comparisons.
The main findings are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ranking.} With the segmentation held fixed, correlating each
        ion image with the GMM posterior maps outperformed IG in 140 of 144
        paired comparisons across three training seeds. The difference was
        statistically supported for every dataset and both VAE arms. With the
        number of segments chosen by BIC instead of the class count, it held
        on CAC and RCC but not on GBM.
  \item \textbf{Spatial information.} In these tests, neighbourhood input to
        the encoder gave no consistent, spatially specific gain, while
        Moran's~$I$, a label-free spatial statistic, was the strongest
        label-free ranking on GBM and RCC and within 0.01 of the best on CAC.
  \item \textbf{Encoder.} A linear five-component PCA segmentation performed
        comparably to the VAE, so the nonlinear encoder was not shown to be
        necessary.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility.} msiPL reproduced. The published S\textsuperscript{3}PL
        glioblastoma score lay outside the distribution of ten reproduced
        seeds, and weight initialisation dominated the seed effect in two of
        three tested sections; data order also mattered in one GBM section.
\end{itemize}

The contributions of this report are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a ranking ablation showing that segment-then-correlate outperforms
        gradient attribution in this pipeline, with paired statistics over
        three training seeds, and a check of which part of that result depends
        on a class-informed number of segments;
  \item evidence that a linear five-component PCA segmentation performs
        comparably to the VAE, so the nonlinear encoder was not shown to be
        necessary;
  \item controlled tests of spatial context at the encoder, segmentation and
        ranking stages, with dataset-specific ranking outcomes;
  \item a statistical account of the S\textsuperscript{3}PL reproduction gap and
        its seed sensitivity;
  \item a matched-budget evaluation protocol with reproduced baselines and
        model-free references, released with the code.
\end{enumerate}
