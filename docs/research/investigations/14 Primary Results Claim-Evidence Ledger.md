# Primary results: claim–evidence ledger

Status: 8 October 2026. This is a map from candidate report claims to saved evidence, not a declaration that the proposed spatial model succeeds. The [mechanistic spatial-context ledger](13%20Spatial%20Context%20Claim-Evidence%20Ledger.md) covers the attention interventions in more detail. Numerical comparisons below use **mSCF1**, the unweighted mean of mixed-class F1 at PCC thresholds 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6; higher is better. The unit in section-level comparisons is a tissue section, not an independent patient. All primary VAE comparisons below use seed 1 unless explicitly stated.

The latest raw evidence commits before this synthesis are `0e464e8` (remaining GBM S3PL seed screen) and `958926c` (runtime repeats). They identify the reviewed checkout, **not** a verified cluster revision for every historical run. Saved configurations, checkpoint hashes and logs are stronger per-run provenance where present. Do not substitute a local revision for missing run provenance.

## What may be said now

The safest concise result is: **this VAE/GMM/IG pipeline improves matched-budget peak scores over the reproduced legacy msiPL pipeline on both collections, but the isolated benefit of its tested spatial context is collection-dependent and not spatially specific under the controls. Matched-count S3PL remains stronger on CAC; its GBM paper score was not reproduced, and the executable 10-epoch GBM baseline is initialization-sensitive at section level.** This statement is descriptive, not proof of a causal mechanism or patient-level generalisation.

For the final report, keep reconstruction, peak-selection mSCF1, runtime, and memory as separate outcomes. Do not call a smaller total runtime “faster training” without stating the epoch and workflow boundaries. Do not use exploratory outcome-selected sections or mask-stratified diagnostics as held-out evidence. The next synthesis should show per-section effects and patient grouping, then decide whether a further controlled S3PL or attention test could change the principal conclusion.
